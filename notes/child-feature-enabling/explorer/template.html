Does an easy root contrast teach the feature a hard one needs?

Two tools for the child-feature-enabling study on tree tensor networks. The first integrates the planted model's gradient flow in your browser. The second replays the saved depth-four runs in which the easy head's gradient into the lower cores was cut.

Planted model: one sign per child, a root that needs their product

The left child hides a sign U and the right child a sign V. The easy target is U and the hard target is UV. Each child table starts with alignment δ to its sign. The ceiling Ah = rL²rR² is the most a refitted root could explain with the children frozen, and Dh is what the trained root explains.

Depth-four grammars: cutting the easy head's gradient

Two heads read the same product features of a 16-leaf tree and fit two contrasts of the root label: the one an additive function of the true child symbols explains best (easy) and the one it explains worst (hard). In the intact arm both errors train the lower cores. In the detached arm the easy head still trains, but its error no longer reaches the lower cores. Both arms of a pair share their starting weights and their batches.
