# lm-bytes: supervisor report

This experiment ran to completion as defined, with no amendments, and H1 is **supported**. All 40 planned runs finished on one A40 in 1.86 h of training, and the worker cost about $1.13 in total. The results are in [README.md](README.md). The pre-registered verdicts are in the Outcome section of [EXPERIMENT.md](EXPERIMENT.md).

## What ran

1. **Launch.** `orch experiment launch lm-bytes` at 21:43:53Z created worker `e77qu59iyyx1ik`, an NVIDIA A40 in CA-MTL-1 at $0.59/h. The launcher skipped the L40S, because 10 h at $1.09/h would exceed the $10 budget, and no RTX A6000 was in stock.
2. **Checks.** `nvidia-smi`, the CUDA assertion and `studies.lm.checks` ran as one `orch exec` step. Every check passed in 8.4 s, and the GPU reported torch 2.11.0+cu128 on the A40.
3. **Training.** `run.py --hours 8 --procs 3` ran as its own background `orch exec` step. It finished all 40 runs in 1.86 h: 10 LR pilots, the 15-run main comparison, the rest of the plan and the two n-gram fits. No run was skipped or diverged, and the deadline never came close.
4. **Analysis and plots.** `analyze.py` and `plot.py` ran on the worker as their own steps.
5. **Finish.** `orch finish` published the results, merged the folder (commit `524e0cd`) and deleted the worker at 23:38:28Z. The eight r = 32 TTN checkpoints went to `artifacts/lm-bytes/ttn_r32_s0/` on the network volume. `orch ls` showed no lm-bytes worker left.

## Decisions I made

- **The order of the checks step.** I ran the definition's first four commands (`nvidia-smi`, the CUDA check, `mkdir`, the checks) as one `orch exec` step called `checks`, under `set -euo pipefail`. I ran training, analysis and plotting as separate steps. The commands were executed exactly as written.
- **The figure legend.** In the worker's `params.pdf`, the legend covered the frozen-fusion TTN's markers. I changed `plot.py` to make the figure wider and put the legend outside the axes, then redrew both figures on the coordinator from the saved results. No data or numbers changed. `curves.pdf` was regenerated by the same script, but its content is the same.
- **Reading the transformer comparison.** I reported the rule's verdict, "equal". I didn't upgrade it to "TTN better" because of the consistent seed ordering, which the README states separately.
- **The fact-check.** The fact-checker found 15 claims in the first draft that didn't fully hold. I applied all of them. The main ones were:
  - "Most of what the TTN learns comes from training the fusion cores" was wrong. Training them is worth 0.94 bits/byte, which is 14% of the gain over uniform and 30% of the gain over the KN unigram.
  - The r = 64 TTN is also within the margin of KN order 8.
  - The r = 128 TTN vs KN order 8 ordering flips between validation and test.
  - The pre-training checks were overstated.
- **The result label.** I recorded `supported`, because H1 is the only directional hypothesis and it met its criterion. The open comparisons are reported as they came out.

No amendments were needed, and none of the "If something fails" branches triggered.

## What the user should do

- **Read the chain result.** The matched chain beats the TTN by 0.073 bits/byte with no seed overlap. That goes against the hierarchy story, and it is the main thing the mentor will want to discuss.
- **Decide on follow-ups.** Each of these needs a new definition:
  - an LR pilot at 3e-3, because every family chose 1e-3, the top of the two-point grid;
  - a no-clip or clip-at-10 rerun, because clipping at 1.0 binds on 95–100% of steps for the tensor networks;
  - the chain against the TTN at 160k updates.
- **Update the shared log.** I left `EXPERIMENTS.md` and any shared notes alone, as instructed. The planning session should add this run to the experiment log.
