# Width on the root task: what the saved runs answer, and what would need a new run

Follow-up of 2026-11-19 to [README.md](README.md), root task only. Nothing was trained on a GPU for it. It uses the saved `rhm-dynamics` and `rhm-controls` results, the three width-64 trajectories of the 1e-3 rerun (taken from the published run archive), and CPU replays with [`norms.py`](norms.py).

**The saved runs answer the width question for final accuracy and leave the speed question bounded by the schedule.** Widths 8, 16 and 32 end at test accuracy 1.0000 in 3 of 3 grammars and never fall below 0.93 after first reaching 0.9. Width 64 reaches 0.9 earliest and is unstable at the shared peak learning rate of 3e-3: all three root runs fall below 0.9 again at some logged evaluation, and one of them (grammar 2) ends at chance. At 1e-3 the same three runs reach 0.9 at the same update and end at 1.0000 with no dip. This establishes that the width-64 runs are sensitive to the learning rate. It does not establish why.

One run per cell (init seed 0), so every entry is a single trajectory. The diagnostic set is evaluated every 50 updates up to 3,000 and every 1,000 after that, so a late dip shorter than 1,000 updates can be missed, and "lowest logged accuracy" is an upper bound on the true minimum.

Three readings of the table:

- **Time to 0.9 and final accuracy come apart only at width 64.** At 3e-3 the width-64 run on grammar 2 is the fastest to 0.9 (with the two others) and the only run that ends at chance. Counting its early crossing as "faster" and its end point as a separate failure, as the definition's rules did, is the right bookkeeping, and the figure shows both.
- **The instability at 3e-3 is in all three width-64 root runs, not one.** Grammar 0 falls to 0.86 at update 2,750, grammar 1 to 0.12 at update 9,000 and recovers, grammar 2 falls to chance at update 6,000 and stays.
- **The speed gain from width is running out before 64.** The crossing time falls by a factor of 2.5 from width 8 to 16, 1.7 from 16 to 32 and 1.2 from 32 to 64, and at width 64 the crossing comes 7 to 28 updates after the end of the 100-update warmup, on a logging grid whose neighbouring points there are 107, 128 and 150. At width 64 the crossing is also the same at 1e-3 and at 3e-3 (128 updates, a third of the summed learning rate: 0.079 against 0.236), so at that width it is not the step size that sets it.

## Tying

The aligned untied tree is the baseline above. For the tied tree the saved evidence is in [`rhm-controls`](../rhm-controls/README.md): on root the tied tree reached the threshold in 183–219 updates against 500–537 for the untied tree, on last-token prediction the order flipped (537–750 against 313–449), and the two tied on final quality in 12 of 12 pairs. The root comparison used separately piloted rates, 3e-3 for the tied tree and 1e-3 for the untied one, and in summed learning rate the two are level (0.40–0.51 against 0.45–0.49).

A matched-rate comparison exists across the two experiments, which share grammars, width 16, identity init and rate 3e-3. The untied tree of `rhm-dynamics` reached 0.9 at 219, 219, 250, 250, 250 and 219 updates for (grammar, init seed) = (0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 0), (1, 1), (2, 0), (2, 1), and the tied tree of `rhm-controls` at 183, 183, 200, 200, 200 and 219. The tied tree is earlier in 5 of 6 pairs and level in one, by 36 to 50 updates, a factor of about 1.2 where the piloted comparison gave about 2.5. The two experiments ran on different GPUs and this pairing was not planned, so it is a descriptive cross-check. It says that most of the root speed difference in `rhm-controls` came from the rate. Whether the remaining factor of 1.2 comes from a tied core receiving the summed gradient of every node of its level is a hypothesis that no saved run tests, and under Adam a summed gradient changes the direction and the signal-to-noise ratio of a step, not its size.

## What the CPU replays show about the width-64 failure

`norms.py` replays a run on the CPU for 10,000 updates with the same configuration, initialization and schedule, and logs per-node activation, gradient, parameter and update norms every 10 updates on the training batch. The CPU data stream differs from the GPU one, so a replay is another sample of the same training process, not the saved run. Replays of the root run on grammar 2 existed for width 64 and width 16 at 3e-3; the replay at 1e-3 was added for this follow-up, so the failing case now has its stable-rate counterpart with the same initialization and data seed. A further replay at 3e-3 with AMSGrad is described at the end of this section.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

*From [`results/norms_summary.txt`](results/norms_summary.txt), written by [`norms_summary.py`](norms_summary.py). Norms are Frobenius norms per node, averaged over the nodes of the level. An episode is a maximal run of consecutive logged batches with loss above 0.5. The loss of a uniform output is log 16 = 2.77.*

- **Neither width-64 replay collapsed for good within 10,000 updates.** The GPU run at 3e-3 fell to the uniform output at update 6,000 and stayed there. The CPU replay at 3e-3 came close 35 times and recovered each time (largest batch loss 2.30, smallest logit norm 4.4). So the replays show the instability and not the terminal event.
- **The lower rate removes almost all of it at fixed width.** From 3e-3 to 1e-3 the number of logged batches with loss above 0.1 falls from 328 to 35 and the episodes from 35 to 2, which puts width 64 at 1e-3 next to width 16 at 3e-3 (17 batches, 1 episode). This agrees with the GPU rerun, where 0 of 3 root runs dipped at 1e-3.
- **An episode is a loss of output scale over tens of updates, not a one-step blow-up.** In the five episodes read in detail (around updates 2,340, 5,960, 6,330, 8,410 and 9,280 of the 3e-3 replay), the logit norm falls from 40–80 to 10–20 over 20 to 40 updates while the batch loss rises above 1, the activation norm at the top level falls by 20 to 60 %, the norm at level 1 changes by less than 10 %, and the logit norm then climbs back over a similar stretch. The uniform output is the end point of the same motion, logits at zero. In a multilinear network that point has vanishing gradients for every core at once, which is where the variance-init runs of README.md stayed, so it would explain why the GPU run did not come back. The replays do not show that step.
- **At 3e-3 the width-64 tree moves scale from the leaves into the cores.** Its level-1 core norm grows by a factor of 8.5 and its leaf norm falls by a factor of 3.1, against 2.8 and 1.4 at 1e-3, and 2.9 and 1.2 for width 16 at 3e-3. The core norm has tripled by update 1,000 (34.3), and the first logged batch with loss above 1 is at update 690, so the drift and the episodes are both present from early on and the replays do not order them.
- **One arithmetic fact fits the rate dependence.** Adam's first step moves every entry of a tensor by the learning rate. A fusion core has r³ entries and, with the identity init, a norm close to √(2r − 1), so that step is about r/√2 times the learning rate relative to the core: 4.0 times larger at width 64 than at width 16 at the same rate (1.36e-3 against 3.43e-4 at the first warmup update), and within a factor of 1.3 of width 16 at 3e-3 when width 64 runs at 1e-3 (4.5e-4). If the size of a dense step relative to the core is what decides stability, the largest stable rate falls as 1/r. This is a statement about update 0 and a hypothesis about the rest.
- **How an episode starts, and the account being tested.** Episodes start from near-zero loss. Around update 2,300, for example, the batch loss goes from below 1e-4 to 0.017 in 10 updates, the gradient norm of the root core from 2e-4 to 0.13, and its relative step from 2.1e-3 to 1.1e-2. Under cross-entropy on a separable target the gradients shrink as the fit improves, Adam's second-moment estimate shrinks with them, and the next batch with an error then gets a step near the full learning rate on every entry. AMSGrad keeps the running maximum of that estimate and removes this one ingredient, so a replay at 3e-3 with AMSGrad (`norms.py --amsgrad`) tests the account on the CPU. It removes part of the instability and not most of it. Batches with loss above 1 fall from 23 to 2 and the largest batch loss from 2.30 to 1.17, with the smallest logit norm at 14.4 in place of 4.4, so the deep excursions nearly disappear. Batches with loss above 0.1 only fall from 328 to 186 and episodes from 35 to 22, far from the 35 and 2 of the 1e-3 replay, and the core norm still grows by a factor of 6.4. On this one replay per arm, the decay of Adam's second-moment estimate accounts for how deep the excursions go and not for how often they start; the rate is the larger lever.

## Proposed runs, for approval

Neither is launched. Each would go through the normal definition (`runpod-research:experiment`) before any worker starts.

**A. Mechanism of the width-64 collapse: the known failing run against its stable counterpart.**

- *Comparison.* Root, grammar 2, width 64, untied, identity init, init seed 0, the original online data seed, on the GPU so that the data stream is the saved run's. Two arms differ only in the peak rate, 3e-3 and 1e-3, both on the 40,000-update cosine schedule. A third arm, 3e-3 with AMSGrad, tests on the saved run's own data stream whether the reduction of deep excursions seen in the CPU replay is enough to prevent the terminal collapse.
- *Logged every update:* batch loss; logit norm and the mean margin (correct logit minus the largest other); activation norm per level; gradient norm, parameter norm and relative update norm ‖Δθ‖/‖θ‖ per level; Adam's step in units of the learning rate, mean |m̂|/√v̂ per level, and the mean √v̂. The diagnostic set every 50 updates for the whole run. Model and optimizer state every 250 updates, and a rolling buffer of the last 20 states at 10-update spacing, so that the last state before a collapse can be reloaded and stepped through.
- *Read-out, fixed before the run:* whether the logit norm falls or rises into the collapse; which level's activation norm moves first; whether Adam's step exceeds the learning rate per entry on the batches that start it; and whether reloading the state 10 updates before the collapse and repeating those updates at a third of the rate avoids it.
- *Stopping rule.* A run stops 500 updates after its diagnostic loss is at or above 2.7 at two consecutive evaluations past update 1,000, and otherwise at update 15,000. The saved run collapsed at update 6,000 and every first collapse in the saved runs came before update 9,000. If the 3e-3 arm has not collapsed by 15,000 the mechanism question is not answered by this run; it is reported as not reproduced and not extended.
- *Cost.* At most 15,000 updates per arm. The saved width-64 runs took 19.5 ms per update with six sharing an A6000; with per-update logging I estimate 30 ms, so 8 minutes per arm, and about half a GPU-hour for the three arms with setup, $0.35 at $0.69 per hour. Proposed cap: 1 hour and $1.50.

**B. A wider sweep, only if A supports a step-size account.** A width above 64 at the shared rate of 3e-3 would add little: the crossing time is already at the end of the warmup at width 64, and the run would be expected to be unstable. What a wider sweep could establish is how the largest stable rate scales with width, which the saved runs bracket at one width only (between 1e-3 and 3e-3 at width 64, at or above 3e-3 at widths 8 to 32). The smallest version is widths 16, 32, 64 and 128 at three rates each around the expected boundary, root only, three grammars and init seeds 0 to 2 at width 64 (which also gives the collapse rate that README.md lists as unchecked), with the diagnostic set logged every 50 updates throughout and the measure "any logged accuracy below 0.9 after first reaching it by update 15,000". Width 128 has 29 million parameters and its time per update is not known, so the run starts with a 200-update timing step and drops width 128 if the projected total exceeds 2 hours. Rough cost: 2 to 5 GPU-hours, $1.50 to $3.50 on an A6000.
